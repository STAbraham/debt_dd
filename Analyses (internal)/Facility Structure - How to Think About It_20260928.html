Debt Facility Structure — How to Think About It

Internal working note · Zed · 2026-09-28 · Draft for Steve. General knowledge, not legal or tax advice — items marked [verify] go to counsel.

1. Why this note exists

Two funds are now talking structure with us, and each has revealed how they think:

  • FPF (Raymond Katz). Committed to indicative terms or a pass after an internal meeting the week of 9/21. His diligence (7/31 list, 8/26 follow-ups, 8/12 and 9/18 calls) has been entirely asset-level: statement and cardholder tapes, cohort losses, charge-off policy, fee mechanics, funds flow. On 9/18 he heard our $5M target and our preference for a PHP onshore facility long-term with an offshore (e.g. Singapore) structure near-term; Singapore would be his first facility there.
  • Accial Capital (Carrie Ruh, email 8/19). Volunteered their standard shape before diligence: 2–4 year tenor with a ~6-month drawdown, a long interest-only period with recycling, amortization matched to asset tenor; fixed commitment with borrowing-base compliance once drawn; Singapore or global holdco preferred (Philippine onshore possible); off-balance-sheet SPV with a corporate guarantee from the holdco; USD.

This note lays out the structural choices those two conversations imply, what each choice costs us, and where we should push. Section 8 is the recommended posture.

2. The underlying problem

Every structure has to reconcile four facts:

  1. The lender's dollars sit offshore (FPF in Panama; Accial lends in USD).
  2. The collateral is PHP credit-card receivables owned by ZFPI, the BSP-regulated issuer.
  3. Our parent is Zed Financial, Inc. (Delaware). There is no Singapore entity today; we would form one only if a lender's structure needs it.
  4. Cash must cross the Philippine border in both directions: funding in, debt service out.

Three decisions define any proposal: who borrows (§3), how the lender reaches the receivables (§4), and therefore what product it actually is — asset-backed or venture debt (§5). Tax (§6) and balance-sheet treatment (§7) follow from those three.

Both funds gravitate offshore for the same reason: an English-law-style jurisdiction with no FX controls, quick enforcement, and a treaty network. Singapore is the default home for that piece; it is not required for FPF, which has never done a deal there.

4. How the lender reaches the receivables — the recourse ladder

ZFPI owns the receivables. Whatever entity signs the loan, the lender's real position is set by which rung it sits on. Strongest first:

  1. True sale to an SPV. ZFPI sells receivables arising on designated accounts; the SPV borrows senior from the fund and we hold the junior first-loss piece; collections run through a waterfall (servicing fee → fund interest → fund principal → our residual). Legally isolated from a ZFPI insolvency. Hard with Philippine card receivables: the account stays with the licensed issuer; documentary stamp tax and VAT on transfers, withholding on the interest component paid offshore, and "doing business / lending without a license" questions for a foreign SPV [verify]. Onshore route under the Securitization Act needs an SEC-approved plan. Legal cost likely well into six figures — heavy for $5M.
  2. ZFPI grants security directly to the lender. Upstream guarantee plus a security interest over receivables and collection accounts (registered under the Personal Property Security Act, held through a Philippine security trustee). Strongest position short of a sale. Check corporate-benefit rules for upstream guarantees and BSP limits [verify].
  3. Limited-recourse secured intercompany loan, assigned to the lender. ZFPI secures a loan from the borrower (parent or SG SPV) with the pool; the borrower assigns that secured note to the fund. On default the fund steps into the lender-of-record position against ZFPI. Common in EM fintech deals; gives SPV economics without a true sale. Carrie's 8/19 description (offshore SPV, onshore security, holdco guarantee) reads as this rung.
  4. Share pledge over ZFPI only. The fund ranks behind every ZFPI creditor. This is venture debt regardless of what the term sheet calls it.

On any rung below 1, a Philippine rehabilitation (FRIA) stay order on ZFPI can freeze enforcement. Lenders price that with tighter covenants, a fuller guarantee, and sometimes warrants.

5. What product it is: asset-backed vs venture debt

The borrower label does not decide this. The features do:

Asset-backed (borrowing base)Venture debt
SizingAdvance rate × eligible receivables (e.g. 80–85% of balances <30 DPD); grows with the bookMultiple of equity raised or of runway
SecurityPerfected lien on receivables and collection accounts (rungs 1–3)Blanket lien on the company (rung 4)
ProtectionsEligibility criteria, delinquency and excess-spread triggers, early amortizationCash covenants, MAC clause, fixed amortization
Lender upsideUsually noneWarrants

Both funds are offering asset-backed: Accial says "borrowing-base compliance once drawn"; Raymond has diligenced nothing but the assets. That is also the better product for us — it scales with the book, does not depend on equity runway, and gives a principled basis for refusing warrants. The one question that settles it with Raymond: does he size the facility off the borrowing base or off the company?

6. Tax map [verify all with counsel]

6.1 Interest leaving the borrower

  • From Delaware to a foreign fund. Default 30% US withholding; no US–Panama income-tax treaty (information exchange only), so it is either the portfolio-interest exemption (0%) or 30%. The exemption fails if the lender is a 10% shareholder (warrants count as if exercised), if any interest is contingent on our revenue, profits or portfolio performance, or if the lender is a bank lending in its ordinary course. Loan docs will gross us up, so the risk of failure is ours. Interest deduction is worth little while the parent is loss-making (adds to NOLs; 163(j) cap should not bite at our size).
  • From ZFPI to an offshore lender. 20% by default; ~15% under the PH–US or PH–Singapore treaty with a BIR treaty-relief filing; no treaty relief for Panama.
  • From a Singapore SPV to the fund. 15% unless a treaty or exemption applies (a Singapore–Panama treaty exists; rate to confirm). Singapore has a tax-incentive scheme for securitisation SPVs. The SG entity can be disregarded for US tax to avoid CFC issues on the spread.

Pledging ZFPI shares from a corporate parent no longer triggers a meaningful Section 956 deemed dividend (post-2019 regs), and it is capped by ZFPI's accumulated earnings, which are negative.

7. On- vs off-balance-sheet, and the guarantee

  • On-balance-sheet (rungs 2–4, any borrower): fast and cheap to document; no sale, so the cardholder and BSP relationship is untouched; the fund carries our corporate risk as well as the asset risk and prices accordingly.
  • Off-balance-sheet (rung 1): the fund underwrites the pool, not our burn, so advance rates improve over time; debt is non-recourse to the parent, which sits cleanly beside equity and venture debt in the Series B story.
  • What "off-balance-sheet" actually means. Because we hold the first loss and service the assets, we will almost certainly consolidate the SPV (IFRS 10 / US GAAP VIE rules) — the debt still appears on the consolidated balance sheet. What we gain is legal isolation and non-recourse treatment, not accounting presentation.
  • The guarantee is the real negotiation. A full holdco guarantee (Accial's ask) gives the non-recourse benefit back. Standard negotiated position: guarantee limited to servicer performance, reps and warranties, and fraud ("bad-boy" carve-outs) — no guarantee of credit losses. On a Delaware-borrower structure the parent guarantee is inherent, so the fight moves to negative pledges and how the parent's other debt ranks.

8. Our constraints and the recommended posture

What we want (as told to Raymond 9/18): a ~$5M first facility closed before the Series B process (conversations ~end 2026, close mid-2027); terms that keep unit economics positive after cost of financing; PHP onshore long-term because USD debt against PHP assets is an FX mismatch we carry (the ~36% asset yield gives cushion, not immunity); offshore near-term if that is what gets the first fund in.

Posture

  1. Insist on asset-backed mechanics whoever the borrower is — borrowing base, eligibility criteria, performance triggers — and on rung 2 or 3 collateral. Do not accept a share-pledge-only structure dressed as a facility.
  2. Resist warrants on two grounds: the product is asset-backed, and warrants can void the portfolio-interest exemption on a Delaware borrower.
  3. Default to the simplest borrower that works: the Delaware parent with a secured, mirrored intercompany note assigned to the fund (rung 3). No new entity, possibly 0% US withholding, closes fastest.
  4. Offer the Singapore sister SPV if the lender needs isolation or enforcement (Accial will; FPF may not) — and in exchange ask for a limited guarantee and a written path to true-sale treatment when the facility passes ~$10–15M. The borrowing-base plumbing carries over unchanged.
  5. Do not pay for a true-sale securitisation at $5M. Put migration language in the docs instead.
