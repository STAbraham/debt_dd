The facts every structure must reconcile: the lenders' dollars sit offshore (FPF in Panama; Accial lends USD); the collateral is PHP credit-card receivables owned by ZFPI, a BSP-supervised non-bank card issuer; our parent is Zed Financial, Inc. Three decisions define any proposal: who borrows (§3), how the lender reaches the receivables (§4), and therefore what product it is — asset-backed or venture debt (§5). Tax (§6) and balance-sheet/guarantee (§7) follow. §8 shows what peers have done; §10 is the recommended posture.

2. Basics — the concepts behind the jargon

What a lender owns. A loan is an unsecured promise to pay. If the borrower fails, the lender stands in line with every other unpaid creditor and shares what is left. Security (collateral) is how a lender jumps the line: a legal interest in a specific asset that it can take or sell on default and be paid from first. The structure debate is about which asset the lender gets that first claim on, and how many other claims stand between the lender and the cash that actually comes from cardholders.

Ranking. On insolvency, claims are paid in fixed order: secured creditors from their collateral, then unsecured creditors pro rata, then shareholders. "Ranks alongside" means shares pro rata. Each legal entity has its own queue: ZFPI's creditors are paid from ZFPI's assets; the Delaware parent's creditors from the parent's assets. The parent does not own the receivables — it owns shares of ZFPI, which are worth only what remains after ZFPI's own creditors are paid.

Share pledge (share charge). The parent gives the lender a security interest in the ZFPI shares it holds. On default the lender can take or sell the shares — i.e. take control of ZFPI as a company. It is not a claim on the receivables: the lender becomes ZFPI's owner, and owners are last in ZFPI's own queue. If ZFPI is itself insolvent — the moment a lender most needs its security — the shares can be worth nothing. A share pledge protects against the parent misbehaving, not against the assets failing. That is why a share-pledge-only deal is venture debt whatever the term sheet calls it.

Why an SPV changes the ranking picture. If FPF lends to the Delaware parent, it sits in the parent's queue with every other parent creditor — a later venture-debt bank, a litigant, an unpaid vendor. The documents fight over that (negative pledges, intercreditor agreements), but FPF's risk is "Zed the company." If FPF lends to a special-purpose vehicle whose only asset is its claim on a ring-fenced receivables pool and whose only creditor is FPF — the documents forbid it from borrowing elsewhere, hiring, or doing any other business — there is no queue. Nothing that happens at the parent puts a competing claim inside the SPV. FPF's risk is "the pool, plus the mechanics that move cash from ZFPI to the SPV." That is isolation, and it is why lenders accept a thinner guarantee and a higher advance rate for it. The catch: unless the SPV actually buys the receivables (a true sale), ZFPI still owns them and the SPV is merely ZFPI's secured creditor, so a ZFPI insolvency still reaches FPF — with a much shorter queue than a parent-level loan.

"Fast share-charge enforcement under Singapore law." A charge over a Singapore company's shares normally carries a private power of sale: on default the chargee sells or takes the shares without a court order (Conveyancing and Law of Property Act §24 for mortgages by deed; market practice adds blank transfers, irrevocable proxies and undated director resignations so the switch is mechanical). In the Philippines, by contrast, enforcing security is a court-heavy process and a FRIA stay order can freeze it for months. Singapore's reliability makes the offshore layer predictable — if things go wrong, the fund takes the SPV and with it the SPV's secured loan into ZFPI. Two nuances the research turned up: (i) Singapore's restructuring moratorium (IRDA §64) can restrain enforcement of security against the SPV if the SPV itself applies for protection — but a charge granted by the Delaware parent over the SPV's shares is the parent's property and is not stayed by the SPV's moratorium; (ii) IRDA §440 (ipso facto) bars accelerating a loan "by reason only" of the borrower's insolvency filing — plain loan agreements are not carved out — but acceleration on payment or covenant default remains available. Neither speeds up anything inside the Philippines.

Guarantee vs security. Security is a claim on an asset; a guarantee is a third party's promise to pay if the borrower does not. Accial's "holdco corporate guarantee" puts the parent's whole balance sheet back behind the SPV — which gives back most of the isolation benefit. The negotiated middle: the parent guarantees it will not cheat (fraud, misrepresenting the pool, failing to service — "bad-boy" carve-outs) but does not guarantee that cardholders pay.

On/off balance sheet. A legal statement (the debt is not the parent's obligation; the assets are not the parent's) that is often misheard as an accounting one. Under both IFRS 10 and US GAAP (ASC 810), a sponsor that keeps first loss and servicing consolidates the SPV; ASC 860 derecognition additionally requires legal isolation (a true-sale opinion) and ceded control. A secured loan to a wholly-owned SPV is on the parent's consolidated balance sheet under either framework.

Borrowing base. The facility's drawable amount = an advance rate (say 80–85%) × the balance of eligible receivables (e.g. under 30 days past due, within concentration limits), recomputed monthly. If the pool shrinks or sours, the borrower repays down to the new cap. This is what makes a facility "asset-backed" regardless of which entity signs.

4. How the lender reaches the receivables — the recourse ladder

ZFPI owns the receivables. Whatever entity signs, the lender's real position is set by which rung it sits on. Strongest first:

  1. True sale to an SPV. ZFPI sells receivables arising on designated accounts; the SPV borrows senior from the fund, we hold the junior first-loss piece; collections run through a waterfall. Legally isolated from a ZFPI insolvency. Hard here: the card account stays with the licensed issuer; a Securitization Act (RA 9267) route needs an SEC-approved plan and prior BSP endorsement because ZFPI is BSP-supervised; the buyer's discount/yield is subject to 12% VAT or gross receipts tax depending on who the buyer is [counsel — no BIR ruling on card-receivable sales found]; a revolving purchase program run by a foreign SPV raises "doing business in the Philippines" questions under the Foreign Investments Act (isolated transactions are excluded; a continuing program is riskier) [counsel]. Chambers' 2026 Philippines guide: the legal framework exists but "the securitisation market has not yet developed." No Philippine fintech has ever disclosed one (§8). Legal cost well into six figures — heavy for $5M.
  2. ZFPI grants security directly to the lender. Upstream guarantee plus a security interest over receivables and collection accounts. Now materially easier than a year ago: the Personal Property Security Act registry (PPSR) went live 3 Feb 2025, perfection is by registered notice, and the Act allows direct collection of receivables (§48) and commercially reasonable private disposition (§49) without court process. Upstream guarantees are permitted if authorized in the articles and for demonstrable corporate benefit (the proceeds being on-lent to ZFPI supplies it); no statutory financial-assistance prohibition. Whether ZFPI's BSP authority as a non-bank card issuer restricts encumbering receivables: [counsel — nothing found in RA 10870 / Circular 1003; likely notification rather than consent].
  3. Limited-recourse secured intercompany loan, assigned to the lender. ZFPI secures a loan from the borrower (parent or SG SPV) with the pool; the borrower assigns that secured note to the fund. On default the fund steps into the lender-of-record position against ZFPI. This is the mechanism visible in the only public Philippine security package (Salmon's bond terms: share pledges over the PH companies + pledged intragroup loans) and matches Carrie's 8/19 description and Accial's "recourse to the originator" language.
  4. Share pledge over ZFPI only. The fund ranks behind every ZFPI creditor. Venture debt.

On rungs 2–4 a Philippine rehabilitation Commencement Order (FRIA §16) suspends enforcement of security; the lien is not impaired (§60) and the court may permit foreclosure if the asset is not needed for rehabilitation, but the delay is real. Lenders price it with tighter covenants, fuller guarantees and sometimes warrants.

Both funds are offering asset-backed (Accial: "borrowing-base compliance once drawn"; Raymond has diligenced nothing but the assets). It is also the better product for us: it scales with the book, does not depend on equity runway, and gives a principled basis for refusing warrants — which matter doubly on a Delaware borrower because warrants count toward the 10%-shareholder test that would void the portfolio-interest exemption (IRC §318(a)(4)). The one question that settles it with Raymond: does he size off the borrowing base or off the company?

6. Tax map — verified

6.1 Interest leaving the borrower

  • Delaware → foreign fund. Statutory 30% withholding (IRC §881(a), §1442). No US–Panama income-tax treaty (TIEA only, in force 2011), so it is the portfolio-interest exemption (0%) or 30%. Conditions (§881(c)): note in registered form; W-8BEN-E (W-8IMY plus partner-level forms if the fund is a partnership — the 10% test then runs at partner level); lender not a 10% voting shareholder after §318 attribution (options and warrants count as owned); interest not contingent on our receipts, income, profits or asset values (fixed or SOFR-based is fine); lender not a bank lending in the ordinary course (a private credit fund is generally fine); not a related CFC. Standard LSTA/LMA gross-up clauses put the cost of failure on us. Interest deduction is worth little while the parent is loss-making; §163(j) does not apply — the small-business exemption threshold is $31M (2025) / $32M (2026) of aggregated group gross receipts. Section 956: for a corporate US shareholder post-2019 regs, a pledge of ≥⅔ of ZFPI's voting stock is only a problem to the extent ZFPI has positive earnings & profits — zero while it carries a deficit; a profitable year creates exposure to watch.
  • ZFPI → offshore lender. 20% final withholding (NIRC §28(B)(5)(a)); 15% under PH–US (Art. 12) or PH–Singapore (Art. 11) treaties; nothing for Panama. Treaty rate can be applied at source if the payee delivers a tax residency certificate + BIR Form 0901 before payment and we file a Request for Confirmation with BIR-ITAD within four months of year-end (RMO 14-2021 / RMC 77-2021); otherwise 20% is withheld and the payee claims a refund. The PH–SG treaty denies the rate on related-party excess interest and BIR scrutinises conduit SPVs for beneficial ownership.
  • Singapore SPV → fund. 15% final (ITA §43(3), via §45) unless treaty: SG–Panama DTA Art. 11(2) = 5% (0% only for government or bank-to-bank interest). The treaty carries the MLI principal-purpose test from 2022 — an SPV inserted mainly for treaty access is challengeable, so the isolation/enforcement rationale must be genuine and documented. Panama's forthcoming economic-substance rules (expected FY2027) are a watch item. The ASPV scheme (ITA §13M) is irrelevant: it requires ≥S$20M of asset-backed securities, charitable-trust ownership and sunsets 31 Dec 2028.

6.3 Product constraint that shapes the economics

BSP Circular 1165 (Feb 2023) caps card finance charges at 3%/month (36% p.a.), installment add-on interest at 1%/month, cash-advance fees at P200 — our yield ceiling is regulatory, so the facility's all-in cost plus withholding gross-up plus FX hedge must fit under it. This is the "terms that keep unit economics positive" test in concrete form.

7. On- vs off-balance-sheet, and the guarantee

  • On-balance-sheet (rungs 2–4, any borrower): fast and cheap to document; no sale, so the cardholder and BSP relationships are untouched; the fund carries our corporate risk as well as asset risk and prices accordingly.
  • Off-balance-sheet (rung 1 only, and only with third-party first loss and ceded control): the fund underwrites the pool, not our burn; debt is non-recourse to the parent. As §2 explains, a captive SPV we service and hold first loss in will consolidate under both IFRS and US GAAP; what we gain is legal isolation and non-recourse treatment, not presentation.
  • The guarantee is the real negotiation. A full holdco guarantee (Accial's ask) gives the isolation benefit back. Negotiated standard: guarantee of servicer performance, reps and warranties, and fraud — not of credit losses. On a Delaware-borrower structure the parent guarantee is inherent, so the fight moves to negative pledges and how later parent debt ranks.

10. Our constraints and the recommended posture

What we want (as told to Raymond 9/18): a ~$5M first facility closed before the Series B process (conversations ~end 2026, close mid-2027); terms that keep unit economics positive after cost of financing, withholding gross-up and FX hedge — all under the 36% regulatory yield cap; PHP onshore long-term (archetype C is now a proven route); offshore near-term if that gets the first fund in.

Posture

  1. Insist on asset-backed mechanics whoever the borrower is — borrowing base, eligibility criteria, performance triggers, controlled collection accounts — and on rung 2 or 3 collateral perfected on the PPSR. No share-pledge-only structure.
  2. Resist warrants: the product is asset-backed, and warrants can void the portfolio-interest exemption on a Delaware borrower.
  3. Default to the Delaware parent as borrower with a secured, mirrored intercompany note assigned to the fund (rung 3). Cheapest on tax (≈15% total leakage vs ≈20%+ via Singapore), no new entity, fastest to close. Price the 15% PH withholding into the intercompany rate and confirm FPF's vehicle can deliver a clean W-8.
  4. Offer the Singapore sister SPV when a lender needs isolation (Accial will; FPF may not) — it is the market-standard shape (§8 archetype A) and costs ~S$5K/yr plus ~5 points of extra withholding. In exchange: limited (bad-boy) guarantee, and no true-sale plumbing at $5M — migration language only.
  5. Build the borrowing-base reporting once so it carries to a PHP bank line (archetype C) when the book and the PPSR track record justify it.

Questions that change the analysis

  • For Raymond: borrowing base or company sizing? Which FPF entity lends and where is it tax-resident (drives 0% vs 30% US, 5% SG)? What does Singapore solve for FPF that Delaware does not?
  • For Accial: how much of the holdco guarantee is negotiable, and does a limited-recourse loan into ZFPI (rung 3) satisfy their "bankruptcy-remote vehicle"?
  • For us: any other debt planned at the parent before the Series B? Appetite to run an SG entity for a $5M line? FX-hedge budget (peers cite ~1–1.15%/yr)?
