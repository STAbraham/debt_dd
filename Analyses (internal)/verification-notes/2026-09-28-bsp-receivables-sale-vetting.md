# BSP / PH law: can ZFPI sell or pledge card receivables to a foreign SPV? (agent report, 2026-09-28)

Method: primary-text verification (RA 10870, RA 9267, RA 11057, RA 10173, RA 8556, RA 9474, RA 7042, Civil Code, SC decisions; BSP Circular 1003, 1160, 1165, 1048; MORNBFI 2023/2025; MORB 2025; BSP FX Manual May 2025; LRA PPSR circulars). Law-firm commentary, SEC-OGC opinions and NPC circular texts could NOT be retrieved — treat those points as unverified.

1. Card-issuer rules (RA 10870; Circ. 1003; MORNBFI Sec. 101-CC): no prohibition, approval or notification on sale/assignment/securitisation of receivables; only post-licensing MB approval is voluntary cessation of the card business. No borrowing/leverage/encumbrance limit for a non-bank card issuer (contrast pawnshops Sec. 201-P). Collection-agency endorsement needs 7-business-day cardholder notice (RA 10870 §21); servicing SPV-owned receivables is not outsourcing by ZFPI. Circular 1160 unfair-term: assignment "to the detriment of the consumer without consent" — add consumer-neutral assignment clause.
2. Securitization Act RA 9267: §9 BSP endorsement applies to BSP-supervised originators only if the Act is used; Act keyed to PH SEC-registered SPE + SEC Plan + ABS issuance; private bilateral sale to foreign SPV funded by a loan is outside it (no exclusivity clause) — loses §12 true-sale safe harbour and §§28–31 VAT/DST exemption. Bank/QB securitisation rules (MORNBFI 622-Q / MORB 628) don't reach ZFPI unless subsidiary/affiliate of a PH bank/QB. RA 9182 SPV Act irrelevant (NPLs, closed).
3. Assignment (Civil Code 1624–1627): debtor consent not required, notice only affects discharge (Ledonio 2007; South City Homes 2001; Sesbreño 1993); avoid conventional-subrogation drafting (Licaros 2001); public instrument for third-party effect (Art. 1625); PPSA: buyer of receivables is a secured party, anti-assignment clauses ineffective (§10(a)), perfection by PPSR registration, no debtor notice.
4. Data: RA 10870 §16 disclosure exceptions do not cover a purchaser/financier → rely on consent in T&Cs ("assignees, transferees, financiers and their agents"); RA 10173 §21 accountability, no cross-border approval regime, DSA/outsourcing agreement suffices; NPC Circ. 2022-04 registration for ZFPI; anonymised tape outside PI, pseudonymised IDs inside. RA 1405 not relevant.
5. Foreign SPV status: FIA §3(d) continuity test — offshore lender not doing business; revolving purchaser weaker (mitigants: single counterparty, offshore execution, undisclosed assignment). RA 8556 financing company (factoring) requires SEC registration + ≥40% Filipino ownership — 100% foreign SPV can't qualify, so collapses into doing-business question. SPV not an "issuer"/"acquirer" under RA 10870.
6. FX Manual: loan route clean (§§22–25; Notice within 1 month; registration for AAB FX; peso loan from non-resident is still a "foreign loan"). Sale route: no provision for sale of domestic receivables to non-residents; nearest fit §35 "other investments" registered under §36.1(g) (Form W, within 1 year) → §38 repatriation; unregistered peso collections cannot be converted via AABs. Recourse/deferred price risks recharacterisation as a foreign loan. §31.2 if SPV issues peso notes offshore with ZFPI guarantee.
7. Secured-loan alternative: no BSP restriction for non-bank card issuer; quasi-banking trap (Sec. 201-N) at 20+ lenders; PPSR registration.
8. Precedents: no PH consumer-receivables true sale found (BillEase, Atome, Salmon, Home Credit, Tala all loans/bonds); absence of evidence given no search budget.

Bottom line: true sale to a foreign SPV is permitted with notifications only (outside RA 9267, as Civil Code assignment); secured-loan route cleaner; biggest practical obstacle is FX repatriation (+ DST/VAT leakage, doing-business exposure). Not found: SEC opinion on foreign receivables purchasers; commentary confirming RA 9267 optional; NPC circular texts; FX Manual Appendix 1; any PH true-sale precedent.
